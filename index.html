Hi!

I'm Lillianna (she/her). I'm a proud first gen student in the second year of my MA in the Department of Linguistics at the University of Kansas, where I am advised by Dr. Lacey Wade. I am interested in sociolinguistics, sociophonetics, and raciolinguistics. I am specifically interested in how Asian/Asian American* Kansans negotiate their identity, relationships, and place through local Kansas speech features. I am affiliated with the Sociolinguistics Lab and the Phonetics Lab. I also co-run the Queer Linguistics reading group.

Current research

  • Asian/Asian American Kansans

    This work aims to understand how Asian/Asian American Kansans use non-local and local Kansas speech features (e.g. TRAP retraction, PIN-PEN merger, devoicing) to negotiate their identity, relationships, and place.

  • Can Asian Americans sound "Country"?: A matched guise study

    This project investigates how American listeners perceive “Accentedness” and “Countryness” when presented with visual cues, specifically phenotypically Asian and White faces.

  • Analyzing anti-trans legislation in the U.S.

    This research aims to analyze anti-trans legislation in the United States, especially the massive surge of anti-trans bills in recent years, using the discourse-historical approach of critical discourse studies.

Note about the term Asian/Asian American*

I acknowledge that the term Asian/Asian American is a highly politcal term that often misrepresents the abundance of identities, cultures, and communites that potentially fall under the broad category of Asian/Asian American. I have chosen to use this term for my research, but welcome any critiques that may help guide me in my research and better support my community!
